The plan takes the right grammar — pedal, sus colour, phrase-locked layers, failures that do not escalate — and then keeps too many things moving at once. These scores stay listenable because almost nothing moves.

**Missing**

Frequency partition. One wet room only works if the parts do not share a band. A 16-step resonant arpeggio, detuned-saw brass, bass pulses and a long hall all sit in 200Hz–2kHz and turn into the drone you already removed. Carve it: pulse mono under 150Hz, pad roughly 200–900Hz, signals above that with a shorter send. Put the hall on a darkened aux (about 40ms predelay, low-pass on the return), not as a wash on every voice.

Subtraction. Both scores are defined by what drops out. This plan only adds notes, accents, drums, filter and width. After an hour the full tier is the new normal and "busy" stops meaning anything. Cap the layers, and let idle reach near-silence.

A single moving part. A static pedal with one line moving is the whole trick. Here the arpeggio steps, the filter sweeps, the delay smears, the brass swells and the tiers riser. Harmony that avoids V–I still cadences if every change lands on the same 8-bar downbeat. Vary the phrase (6+2, 5+3), or the grid becomes a clock you wait on.

An event budget. Tool calls poured into the arpeggio quantize into a wall when several sessions fire together. One new note per eighth, extras postponed. Accents have to carry read / edit / command, or that information disappears.

**Attack**

The 16-step arpeggio in Far shore will not survive a work day. A resonant low-pass plus dotted-eighth delay sits in the ear's alert band, and the tails erase the contour that was supposed to encode activity. It is the Stellaris crisis loop with a nicer waveform. Use an eighth-note figure of two or three chord tones per bar. Density means how many bars carry an accent. Save sixteenths for the full tier, lead lane only, with a short dark delay.

Deep field spends its sample budget on what GM fonts do worst — choir, horn, harp — and "erupts" when the room gets busy, turning a state into a jump scare. Keep strings, organ and a sine/saw ostinato. Erupt only on a takeover or a commit.

Tiers are the right structure. The riser is not. A whoosh every time energy crosses into "working" competes with the build and with commits. Crossfade one layer over two bars at the phrase boundary.

The build should be steps, not a sweep. A multi-minute filter climb is a slow lawnmower, and if every turn climbs, length stops being readable. Add one partial at four minutes and another at eight: a fifth under the pedal, then a soft octave. Silent between steps, so you can count them.

A sour note that settles into the texture becomes inaudible, so you no longer know what broke. Stinger once, then one quiet restatement of the same interval every 16 bars. Full again only when the check changes. Never a held tone.

Factions as organ, horn, choir and saw re-orchestrate the room whenever the lead project flips, which the three-minute rule will do often. That is a radio. Keep one timbre. Identity is register and one added interval, two notes at the moment of takeover.

The home motif double-books the commit, which already resolves home with a bell. On a day of many commits it becomes a jingle. Keep the cadence for commits. Spend four notes only on a rare event, such as a deploy.

Dropping into 7/8 because a prompt has waited will sound like the sequencer broke, and it punishes focus for something the ask figure already announced. Restate the ask once per phrase, a little higher, capped at three. Leave the meter alone.

**Own**

1. Silence is a state. The pedal plays only while a turn is in flight. When the room is idle the pulse stops and the hall decays. "Nothing running" is the most useful signal you have, and it is what makes the next entry mean something.
2. Reskin the grammar you already have. Calls, sour intervals, asks and the commit bell already mean something. A new style changes their oscillators. It does not add a second motif language.
3. Width follows session count, dryness follows tier, and neither is a snare. Slew the width over seconds. The full tier gets a short, quiet, bandpassed noise tick on 2 and 4. A gated backbeat for hours belongs to the house mood.

**First build**

The pulse and three layers, without the arpeggio and without risers. If that bed lasts an hour, later ideas have a floor.

Tempo 100. Aeolian pedal, sus2 voicings, nearest-note motion, chord every two bars.

- Pulse: sine plus a triangle an octave up, root around E1–A1. Attack 8ms, decay 160ms, sustain 0. Low-pass near 180Hz. Eighth notes, a few dB louder on beat 1, mono, no delay. Duck the pad about 2dB off each pulse.
- Pad: two saws detuned ±7 cents, high-pass 150Hz, low-pass near 800Hz, attack 2s, release 3s, around −18dB. No sweep.
- Working: a square at about 40% width, the add9 only, on the last eighth of every other bar, around −22dB.
- Full: filtered noise, 30–40ms, bandpass near 2kHz, beats 2 and 4, around −26dB.
- Hall: 40ms predelay, decay near 5s, return low-passed near 4kHz. Wet about 0.3 calm, 0.18 working, 0.12 full.
- Calls and sour intervals stay short sine blips an octave above the pad, drier than the bed.
